For the person who owns the desk

Prepress, in the language of the shop

This is the work between the file a customer sends and a plate, or a sheet on a digital press. The notes below are the job. The three programs are aimed at pieces of it.

What prepress is

A customer sends a PDF. Someone in the shop has to make that file match the stock, the press, and the cutter before it is worth imaging. That stretch of work is prepress. It sits after the estimate and before the press operator hits start: page size, the boxes inside the PDF, how many up on the sheet, marks for bindery, and whether the text can even be searched. If that work is sloppy, the press runs the mistake.

The file usually arrives looking finished. It is not finished until it fits the sheet you will actually run. A business card is not a press sheet. A letter is not a landscape tabloid. A booklet in reader order is not a saddle-stitched signature. Prepress is the translation.

LockStepPDF is the subscription for that translation: one recipe, from the pages you fix through imposition, marks, and a hot folder that runs it again. LockStepOCR is free, for the scan that has no text layer. LockStepLabel is under development for label sheets, and it will be a one-time purchase when it ships. There is nothing to buy for it today.

Why files fail

Most failures are ordinary, and they show up after the file has already been accepted. The boxes are the first. A PDF can carry a media box, a crop box, a trim box, and a bleed box. The picture on screen may be the trim, while the box the next tool uses is the media box, a wide field of white around the art. Step that, and the gutter is a lie. You imposed the margin.

Color is the next argument. A file built in RGB will not separate the way a press that wants CMYK expects. An office PDF, a logo dropped in from the web, a photo still in the camera’s color: any of those can ride into a job that the rest of the form is already building as process color. Preflight reads the file against a profile you can edit. The starting points are Standard Digital Press, Commercial Sheetfed, and Soft Proof. You can read the report without a paid license. Export of that report to JSON or PDF, and the jump from a finding into the fix tool, are Pro. Color on the recipe converts the pages before that check.

Low-resolution images show up when a photo that looked sharp on a phone is placed large on a sheet. The screen forgives it. The press does not. Fonts fail when the type was not embedded and the shop PC substitutes something else, so a line reflows or a letter in a logo changes. Bleed fails when the art stops exactly at the trim. The cutter wanders a hair and leaves a white line down the edge. Grow bleed is the step for art that has none: quick grow scales the page, or edge extend mirrors or stretches the outer edge into the new margin. The grown page keeps a TrimBox, so Grid and Booklet can pack on the finished size afterward. None of this is exotic. It is the list a prepress lead already argues about with the file.

What imposition is

Imposition is how the pieces sit on the sheet the press actually runs. One-up art is the customer’s file. The sheet is yours. On a 12×18 or a 13×19, the same card may repeat many times, with a gutter the cutter can use. A booklet is a different problem: reader order has to become printer spreads, and the thickness of the paper moves the pages.

Grid is step-and-repeat onto a press sheet. Type the rows and columns, or use auto max-fit. Gutters, margins, and a 3 by 3 anchor control the white space. For art that already includes bleed, Grid and Booklet step by the finished trim. A gutter of zero means the trims touch. Bleed hangs into the gutter. Crop marks sit on the trim. Cards, tags, and labels are the everyday case: one finished piece, repeated, with a gutter, not a hand-built page for every position.

Booklet is saddle stitch. Creep is the thickness of one sheet. The cover does not shift. Each sheet inside adds that amount again. Skip it, and the face trim shears the outer pages short while the center pages stick out. Facing pages pack on the finished trim when the art already has bleed. White space uses the same 3 by 3 anchor as Grid.

Folds are a visual guide on the monitor, not a production step: a half fold, Z fold, gate fold, roll fold, tri-fold, and the rest of the common schemes. Mail merge sits on the same desk. A template PDF plus a spreadsheet, placed on the page. A saved recipe remembers the template, so a hot folder can be given only the spreadsheet. The full desk, including units and the order of the steps, is on the LockStepPDF page.

What cutter marks are for

The press does not know where the cutter will strike. Cutter puts marks on the sheet so bindery does. Target every page, fronts, or backs. A registration L, a cross, or round dots at the corners. Marks can be black, grey, white, or a chosen color. Impose first, then add the marks. Without them, the operator is cutting to a line that was never drawn.

Why the file should stay on the shop PC

The file is the job. A customer list, a healthcare mailing, a price that is not public yet, a proof that is not approved. Sending that through someone else’s server so a browser can crop it makes a copy you do not control, and it adds a size limit and a wait. The prepress PC, or the shop server beside it, already has the file. Work there.

LockStepPDF and LockStepOCR run on Windows in the building. Nothing has to upload before you can resize, impose, or make a scan searchable. There is no account on the tool, and no file-size cap from a shared queue. That is the point of keeping the desk on the machine that already holds the job.

How the three programs fit

LockStepPDF is the subscription. Version 3.54 is the current Windows release. The job is one recipe: load the files, fix the pages, stack the prepress steps, watch the result, and save the file or let a hot folder run that same recipe. Three pipelines. Preflight can run with those jobs and sort pass, hold, and fail. You can read a preflight report without a paid license. Export of that report, and the jump from a finding into the fix tool, are Pro. The subscription price, the billing period, and a signup link are not published here. It is not a free-forever license, and it is not sold as a one-time purchase.

LockStepOCR is free, version 2.4. It is its own program. Three pipelines, each with a watch folder and an output folder, write a searchable PDF and keep the original page size. The original is not deleted. Use it when a scan or a flat PDF has no text to search, then bring that file back to prepress. LockStepPDF also has OCR hot folders, separate from those prepress pipelines. A scan dropped there becomes selectable. It is not imposed. The two are not the same installer.

LockStepLabel is under development. The job it is being built for is label work: sheets of labels, a finished size, and the gutter between labels. There is no installer, no price, and no buy button. When it ships, it will be a one-time purchase. Do not plan a live job around it yet. Questions can go to the same desk as the other two tools.
